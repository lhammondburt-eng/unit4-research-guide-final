WRT 110 Unit 4: The Research Essay

Refining Your Topic, Scope, and Arguments

Assignment Parameters

  • Length: 5-6 pages (approximately 1250-1500 words), double-spaced.
  • Sources: Minimum of five (5) peer-reviewed academic sources (e.g., academic journal articles or scholarly monographs).
  • Format: Consistent use of MLA 9th Edition or APA 7th Edition.
  • Goal: Defense of a specific, non-obvious position on a complex debate (not just summary or descriptive writing).

The Stakes & Audience

Your thesis must establish why this debate matters to the broader academic community or public discourse. You are writing for an informed, skeptical academic reader who expects claims to be substantiated by verifiable scholarly evidence and logical reasoning.

Is Your Topic Arguable?

Your essay must be built around a focused, assertive, and directional thesis statement.

1. Is it just a fact?

If everyone universally agrees on your premise, it is not a debate. Your stance must be on an issue open to genuine intellectual debate.

2. Does it rely on personal opinion?

Arguments must be built upon shared, verifiable academic knowledge rather than personal anecdotes or unsupported emotional claims.

3. Does it define the stakes?

Why does this matter? Your thesis must establish the consequences or importance of this debate to the public or academic sphere.

From Broad Tension to Narrow Scope

Topics must be narrow, researchable via peer-reviewed literature, and open to multi-sided debate. Below are examples of how to narrow a broad tension.

Topic Area The Tension / Debate Sample Narrow Thesis Scope
Environmental Policy Economic growth vs. Ecological preservation. Debt-for-Nature Swaps: Canceling sovereign debt in developing nations for rainforest protection.
Artificial Intelligence Tech efficiency vs. Human labor value. Human-Replacement Tax: Taxing corporations that automate >30% of their workforce.
Criminal Justice Retribution vs. Restorative rehabilitation. Restorative Justice: Mandatory community-mediation replacing incarceration for first-time theft.

Body Paragraphs & Counterarguments

Every body paragraph must perform deep analytical work using the MEAL Plan Model.

M - Main Idea

Presents a clear, directional claim that serves as the paragraph's core argument.

E - Evidence

Integrates specific facts, statistics, or quotes from vetted scholarly sources.

A - Analysis (50-60% of words)

Explicitly explains how the evidence proves your main idea and thesis.

L - Link

Synthesizes the argument, connects back to the thesis, and transitions logically.

Mandatory Counterargument

You must devote space to identifying and responding to opposing viewpoints using advanced strategies:

  • Direct Refutation: Demonstrate flawed logic, outdated data, or weak assumptions.
  • Acknowledgement & Qualification: Grant partial validity, but show its limited scope.
  • Concession & Re-Nuancing: Concede genuine force and reframe your thesis.
